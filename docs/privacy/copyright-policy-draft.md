# R1 copyright and review policy — needs legal review

Status: **needs legal review**. Draft operating procedure, 21 November 2026. This document does not claim compliance with a statutory notice-and-takedown regime. Applicable jurisdictions, authorized contact details, mandatory notice/counter-notice declarations, service of notices, deadlines and evidence retention must be approved by counsel before launch. No legal deadline, automatic ban threshold or cash remedy is chosen here.

Creators must explicitly confirm for each upload: “I created this content or have permission to share all of it, including its audio.” The current statement is `R1-originality-v1`. Content retains the authenticated account ID, server timestamp, statement version and upload reservation ID as append-only evidence. This is an attestation, not verification of ownership or a transfer/license of rights. Existing videos without this evidence are flagged; their consent is not backfilled.

The Content-owned rights states are `attested` (statement recorded), `flagged` (screening concern or missing/inconclusive evidence), `claimed` (an unresolved allegation), `removed` (applied copyright takedown), and `restored` (a reviewed restoration). These operational labels do not decide legal ownership. A separate moderation/publication state controls visibility.

Authenticated claimants may identify a video, the allegedly infringed work, their authority to report, and a reason through `/copyright/reports`. The existing `/reports` copyright category also accepts concerns for manual triage. A report changes rights status to claimed and enters the existing assigned report queue; it does not itself remove content or impose a strike. Work references are text, never automatically fetched. Do not submit identification documents or unnecessary contact/financial data in free text.

Only an assigned moderator or supervising administrator with live MFA may remove reported content. The reviewer records a reason. Content becomes private and its copyright status becomes removed. Only the successfully applied copyright takedown counts as a strike; replays, unsuccessful decisions, duplicate allegations and unrelated safety decisions do not count twice. Each removed video/action remains independently traceable. There is no automatic suspension, expiry or escalation threshold in R1; repeat-infringer sanctions need legal/product approval.

The creator may contest the current copyright takedown through `/videos/{video_id}/counter-notices`, attesting to accuracy and giving a reason/evidence. Creator ownership is resolved server-side. A counter-notice attaches to the existing appeal. Ordinary appeals remain available. Neither submission restores the video nor reverses a strike automatically. An assigned independent MFA-verified reviewer, distinct from the takedown reviewer and appellant, may deny or restore with reasons. Restoration reverses only the corresponding strike. Reporter identity, raw fingerprints, storage keys and private evidence are not added to the audit read projection.

Before the manual video queue, the worker computes SHA-256 from the actual uploaded bytes. Exact duplicates, a matching approved audio fingerprint reference, missing attestation, unavailable/inconclusive fingerprinting and active copyright cases require manual review. Hash/fingerprint matches are triage signals, not legal findings. The existing Chromaprint provider extracts and aligns fingerprints against the platform’s confirmed-takedown references. It does not prove ownership or supply a global copyright catalog; match scores are triage signals. The optional safety-screening providers remain separate and can only add manual holds.

Trust is an explicit approved `CopyrightReviewPolicy` in Creators; creator invitation or eligibility does not grant it. **The default sample rate is 100% until policy approval.** As authorized for this task, a clean unsampled upload from an explicitly trusted creator may receive automatic moderation approval under an approved lower sample rate. It stays private until the creator publishes it. Sampling uses a server-secret deterministic HMAC, so request retries cannot reroll the sample. The policy snapshot applies only within the ten-minute upload reservation; delayed jobs revert to manual review. Revoking trust stops new snapshots; outstanding bounded reservations require operational review if immediate revocation is needed. No trust-management or role-management UI is added. Provisioning/versioning trust policies and approved fingerprint references requires an owner-reviewed, audited database change process before activation.

Content keeps append-only local rights/attestation/pre-screen evidence with reasons; Moderation keeps its existing immutable audit outbox plus notices, appeals and attributed decisions. Central audit projection/redaction is unchanged. Retention periods, claimant/creator notifications and delivery channels, identity verification, access/deletion requests, disputes involving licensed music, exceptions/limitations and litigation holds need legal/privacy acceptance. No automated notice emails are sent by this implementation.

No project LICENSE is selected or added. The owner must choose a license separately. A project software license is distinct from creator content permissions and third-party tool licenses.

References for legal review: [WIPO copyright guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/business/copyright), which recommends assessing rights and exceptions with legal advice. Technical reference: [official Chromaprint documentation](https://acoustid.org/chromaprint) and [fpcalc source/options](https://github.com/acoustid/chromaprint/blob/master/src/cmd/fpcalc.cpp). These references do not establish jurisdiction-specific compliance.
